# Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

JIVL is maintained as a technical, engineering-focused open-source project.
We want participation to be a harassment-free experience for everyone,
regardless of experience level, background, or identity.

## Expected Behavior

- Engage in good faith, technical discussion. Critique ideas and code, not people.
- Be direct but respectful in code review and RFC discussion.
- Accept that disagreement about verification policy, rule design, or
  architecture is normal and should be resolved through the RFC process
  described in `docs/rfcs/README.md`, not through personal conflict.
- Respect the project's non-affiliation boundaries: do not represent yourself
  as speaking for OpenJDK, Oracle, Spring, Agent Skills, Anthropic, OpenAI,
  GitHub, SkillsJars, or JVMskills.com when participating in JIVL.

## Unacceptable Behavior

- Personal attacks, harassment, or discriminatory remarks or jokes.
- Publishing others' private information without consent.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, issues, or reviews.
- Attempting to influence a verification outcome through pressure, favors, or
  payment (see `GOVERNANCE.md`, "Rules preventing payment for favorable
  verification outcomes").

## Enforcement

The Lead Maintainer (currently Nityam Jigyasu, @nmj18txstate) is responsible
for clarifying and enforcing standards of acceptable behavior, and may remove,
edit, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, and other contributions that
violate this Code of Conduct. As the project grows a reviewer/maintainer team
(see `GOVERNANCE.md`), enforcement responsibility will be shared under a
documented process.

## Reporting

Report unacceptable behavior by opening a confidential report through the
mechanism described in `SECURITY.md` (for security-adjacent conduct issues)
or by contacting the Lead Maintainer directly through GitHub. Do not open a
public issue for conduct reports involving another individual.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces (issues, pull
requests, discussions, RFCs) and when an individual is representing the
project in public spaces.
